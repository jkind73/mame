# SCU device separation — 2026-10-10

The SCU already had its own device implementation and header in `src/mame/sega/saturn_scu.cpp` and `saturn_scu.h`. This follow-through finishes the driver cleanup and verifies the ownership boundary. Registers and their maps, all three DMA channels, DSP DMA integration, interrupts/acknowledgement, timer counters and timers, reset, clock handling and SCU save-state fields live in `saturn_scu_device`; the DSP is its child CPU device. There are no SCU register handlers, DMA engines, interrupt schedulers or timer-counter implementations in `saturn.cpp` or `stv.cpp`.

The board's `update_cpu_halt`, `scu_halt_w` and `system_halt_w` definitions moved verbatim from `saturn.cpp` to `saturn_bus.cpp`. These combine two independent board hold signals; that composition belongs to the shared bus wiring rather than the SCU's internal implementation. Each moved definition was checked byte-for-byte against the pre-change source. The sanitizer fixture now extracts those methods from their new location. No method behavior, device tags, callbacks, address maps, clocks or saved-state fields changed.

Unused direct SCUDSP includes were removed from the three drivers, obsolete commented-out DSP handler declarations were removed from `saturn.h`, and a dead ST-V DMA-hack assignment was removed. Historical SCU interrupt/timer notes were relocated to the SCU file and explicitly identified as investigation history rather than the current model. The obsolete console-header request to move the SCU was removed; the existing checklist item keeps its original wording.

Remaining SCU references in the drivers are necessary board integration: device instantiation, the register-map attachment, interrupt/DMA signal connections, clock and reset wiring, external-cycle observation, and the saved board hold input. The VDP2 write-slot penalty helper describes its output in SCU clocks but models VDP2 slots; the ST-V voice-pitch note records a game issue. These are not duplicated SCU implementations. Removing the map or physical signal connections would disconnect the device rather than improve its separation.
